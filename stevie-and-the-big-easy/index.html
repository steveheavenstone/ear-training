About

A Crescent City band in the desert

Drawing on the historic sounds of the Crescent City, Stevie and the Big Easy plays a vibrant mix of New Orleans funk, soulful blues, second line and jazz — infectious rhythms, horns, and vocals that celebrate Louisiana's musical heritage with a contemporary twist.

The band is led by Phoenix guitarist, singer and producer Steve Heavenstone, and the book runs straight through the city's catalogue: Professor Longhair and the Mardi Gras Indians standards, Allen Toussaint's Lee Dorsey sides, the Meters' funk, Dr. John's piano blues, the Neville Brothers, and the soul and swamp records that grew out of the same rooms.

It travels as a quartet when the stage is small and expands to eight or more with trumpet, trombone and saxophone when the room wants a parade. Every version plays for dancers.

Stage & sound

What we bring, what we need

Typical — final input list on request

The band brings

  • Backline: guitar amp, bass rig, keyboard and stand
  • Drum kit (or plays house kit on shared bills)
  • Music stands and stand lights for the horns
  • Small-room PA and a mixer if the venue has none

The venue provides

  • Stage or floor space — roughly 16 × 12 ft for the big band, 10 × 8 ft for the quartet
  • Two dedicated 20-amp circuits
  • PA with engineer and monitors for larger rooms
  • Shade or cover for outdoor daytime sets, plus water

Typical inputs

  • 3 vocals (lead, drums, keys)
  • Guitar DI or mic, bass DI, keys DI (stereo)
  • Drums: kick, snare, hats, two overheads
  • Horns: one mic each — trumpet, trombone, sax

Logistics

  • Load-in 90 minutes before downbeat; soundcheck 45 minutes
  • Two 60-minute sets or three 45s, breaks to suit the room
  • Based in Phoenix and travels across Arizona
