Ear Training

Two browser-based ear trainers. No install, no account, no audio files — every sound is generated live in the browser. Works on phones.

Key Sense — Functional Ear Trainer

Hear every note and chord relative to the key. A cadence establishes the tonic, then you identify scale degrees, chord functions, full progressions, or melodies by number. Four to five difficulty levels per mode — from three scale degrees up to full chromatic, from I·IV·V up to inversions in the bass. Three instrument voices, and a stats tab that surfaces your top confusion pairs. Includes a 50-minute session plan.

Ben Monder Call & Response

The app plays a note; you play it back on your instrument within the beat. Get it right and a new note comes. Get it wrong and the same note returns until you nail it — and the note name stays hidden the whole time. Adjustable tempo, mic sensitivity, and optional exact-octave matching.

Before you start
  • Headphones or speakers. Call & Response has an Output setting — switch it to Speakers and the app shortens its note, waits for silence, and enables echo cancellation so it can't score its own note as yours. Headphones are still the cleanest option.
  • Call & Response needs mic permission. Your browser will ask. Nothing is recorded or uploaded; pitch detection runs entirely on your device.
  • Sing before you answer. Both trainers are built around committing out loud first, then tapping. Tapping without singing trains your eyes, not your ears.
